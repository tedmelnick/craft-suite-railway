How to use Craft Suite

Sixteen free creative apps that run right in your browser — no install, no account. Here’s everything worth knowing in five minutes.

Getting started Your files Tips Using Claude Troubleshooting

1Getting started

  1. Use Chrome or Edge on a computer. The apps use your graphics card through a newer browser feature (WebGPU) that those browsers support best. Phones and tablets aren’t a good fit.
  2. Click any app on the main page. It opens in the same tab; open it in a new tab if you want to keep the list handy.
  3. Give it a moment the first time. Each app downloads once (tens of megabytes) and prepares itself for your computer. Most take a few seconds; EffectCraft can take a minute or two. After that, opening it again is much quicker.
Each app on the main page says what it’s similar to — “like Photoshop”, “like Excel” — so you can pick the right one quickly.

2Your files

Your work never leaves your computer. Nothing is uploaded; the site only delivers the apps. That’s private — and it also means a few things are up to you:

  • Work is kept in this browser, on this computer, for this site. Another browser, another computer, or a private/incognito window won’t see it.
  • Export or download anything you want to keep. Use the app’s Save, Save As or Export — the file lands in your Downloads folder like any other download.
  • Clearing your browser’s site data (or “cookies and site data”) for this site deletes work kept in the browser.
  • To open your own files, use the app’s Open command, or drag a file onto the app window.
Rule of thumb: if it matters, export it. Treat the browser as a workspace, not a filing cabinet.

3Tips for a smooth experience

  • Run two or three apps at a time, not ten. They share your graphics card; too many heavy apps at once (especially EffectCraft, FilmCraft and LightCraft) can make the browser struggle.
  • Keep the app’s tab visible while it works. Browsers pause hidden tabs to save power, so long exports or renders can stall in a background tab.
  • After the apps are updated, press Ctrl+Shift+R (Mac: Cmd+Shift+R) in an app’s tab if anything looks off — it makes sure you have the newest version.
  • Keep your graphics drivers up to date. Older drivers are the most common cause of graphics errors.

4Optional: let Claude drive the apps

Every app except Craft Anything can be controlled by Claude, Anthropic’s AI assistant: you ask in plain words (“make a budget table”, “fill in this PDF form”) and it works in the app open in your browser. This is entirely optional — the apps work fully on their own.

To use it you run your own small relay — a tiny server that passes messages between Claude and your browser tab. It’s yours alone, so any costs (your Claude plan, and a small hosting plan such as Railway’s) are on your own accounts. One-time setup takes about ten minutes:

One-time setup

  1. You’ll need: a Claude plan that lets you add custom connectors, a free GitHub account and a Railway account.
  2. Copy the project: open the Craft Suite project on GitHub and click Fork.
  3. Start your relay on Railway: New Project → Deploy from GitHub repo → your fork. In the service’s Settings, set Root Directory to server.
  4. Make up two passwords and add them in the service’s Variables: MCP_TOKEN (32+ random characters) and BRIDGE_TOKEN (16+). Keep them private.
  5. Give it an address: Settings → Networking → Generate Domain. You’ll get something like https://your-relay.up.railway.app.
  6. Connect Claude: in Claude, Settings → Connectors → Add custom connector. URL: your relay’s address followed by /mcp. Header: Authorization: Bearer <your MCP_TOKEN>.

Each time you want Claude’s help

  1. Open an app and click the ⦾ button in the bottom-right corner.
  2. Relay address: replace /relay with your relay’s address (https://your-relay.up.railway.app).
  3. Paste your BRIDGE_TOKEN and press Connect agent. Your browser remembers both next time.
  4. Ask Claude: “What tabs are connected?” — then tell it what you’d like done.

Keeping up to date

New apps and app updates need nothing from you. They appear on this site for everyone, and Claude can drive them through the relay you already have — the relay only passes messages and doesn’t care which apps exist.

The rare exception is a change to the relay itself. You don’t need to watch for it: when your relay is out of date, the ⦾ button shows a small yellow dot and its panel says so. Then click Sync fork → Update branch on your GitHub copy; Railway rebuilds your relay in a few minutes, and you reconnect.

Nothing connects until you press Connect, Claude can only reach the tab you connected, and the panel has a read-only switch and a Disconnect button.

5Troubleshooting

What you seeWhat to do
A blank or frozen-looking app on first openWait a minute — it may still be preparing (EffectCraft especially). Then try Ctrl+Shift+R.
“The graphics device was lost”Close other heavy app tabs, then reload. If it keeps happening, update your graphics driver and make sure hardware acceleration is on in your browser’s settings.
An app looks out of date or half-updatedPress Ctrl+Shift+R in its tab.
My saved work is goneCheck you’re in the same browser, on the same computer, not in a private window, and on this exact site address. Work kept in the browser is erased if the site’s data is cleared — export important files.
A Cloudflare error page (e.g. “1027”)The site hit its free daily limit. It resets every day at midnight UTC (evening in the US); try again after that.
Claude says no tabs are connectedCheck the ⦾ panel shows “agent connected”, that the relay address is yours (not /relay), and keep the tab visible.
